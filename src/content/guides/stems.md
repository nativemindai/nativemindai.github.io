## The short version
An 11-tool test by MusicRadar named the stem splitter built into Logic the winner, and FL Studio and Ableton Suite have one built in too.
So start with the music software you already own, and only reach for another tool when it can't do the job.

## Which one to use when
- **You already have FL Studio, Ableton Suite or Logic:** use the one built in. In FL Studio, right-click a clip in the Playlist (Producer Edition and up). In Ableton Live 12.3 Suite, right-click a clip and choose **Separate Stems to New Audio Tracks**. In Logic, Control-click a region and choose **Processing → Stem Splitter**.
- **Free and offline:** Ultimate Vocal Remover. It runs on your own computer. For vocals, choose **MDX-Net**: it was the best mode for vocals in the test.
- **Cleanest online, no install:** LALAL.AI. It scored highest of the online tools in the test.
- **Split the drums into kick, snare and hats:** Logic 13 (Mac only). Its Stem Splitter now breaks a drum stem into kick, snare, hi-hat, toms, cymbals and other percussion. Not on a Mac? Moises' Pro plan splits drum parts too.
- **Practicing or learning a part:** Moises, on your phone or computer. Its free plan splits the vocals from the backing track.

## Do this
1. Pick your tool from the list above.
2. Split only the stems you need. Ableton and Logic let you tick just the parts you want. On LALAL.AI, every stem type you pick uses minutes: for example, a 4-minute song split into 3 stem types uses 12 minutes.
3. Check the quality. Play all the stems together with every fader at 0 dB and no effects. They should sound almost exactly like the original. If a part is missing or sounds watery, try another tool from the list.
4. Use the stems to study how the song was mixed, practice along with it, or try a remix for yourself.

## Watch out
- **Someone else's song stays theirs.** Splitting a song doesn't give you any rights to it. To release anything built on someone else's stems, you need permission from the people who own the song and the recording. LALAL.AI's terms say you're responsible for how you use what it makes.
- **Free plans have limits.** LALAL.AI's free Starter plan gives you 10 minutes in the slower queue and files up to 200 MB, and only lets you preview the result. Downloading needs a paid plan: Lite is $7.50 a month, billed yearly ($90). Moises' free plan gives you 5 splits a month, up to 5 minutes per song, vocals and backing track only.
- **Ultimate Vocal Remover is fiddly on a Mac.** It's free and open source, but to open it on a Mac you have to switch off a security setting first. MusicRadar's tester called that inadvisable and tested it on Windows instead. If you're on a Mac with Logic, use Logic.
- **Check your edition.** FL Studio's splitter isn't in Fruity Edition. Ableton's is only in Suite, from Live 12.3. Logic is Mac only ($199.99 one time, and Logic 13 is a free update if you already own it).
- **No split is perfect.** Busy, dense mixes leave bits of one part in another stem. Even Ableton's manual says you may hear hi-hats in the Others stem.
